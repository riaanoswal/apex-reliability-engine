APEX — 5 MINUTE DEMO RUN SHEET
==============================

BEFORE THEY WALK IN
  1. start_backend.bat   -> wait for "Application startup complete"
  2. start_frontend.bat  -> wait for "Local: http://localhost:3000/"
  3. Open http://localhost:3000, press Ctrl+0 to reset browser zoom.
  4. Leave the API key box EMPTY. You will fill it in during the demo.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE ASSET  (30 s)
   Point at the header: ISA-95 path GULF_FPSO_01 / TRAIN_02 / CMP-204B.
   "This is a compressor bearing. The data is the Case Western Reserve
    University bearing dataset — real measured vibration, not simulated."

2. THE EVIDENCE  (60 s)
   Point at the lower-left chart.
   "That is the envelope spectrum. The tallest peak sits exactly on the
    marker at 105.9 Hz, which is the ball-pass frequency of the outer race
    for this bearing at this speed. The defect frequency is not something
    we chose — it falls out of the bearing geometry."
   Point at the KPI strip: kurtosis ~20 means impulsive, not noise.

   IF ASKED why the velocity is only 0.4 mm/s and Zone A:
   "That is the honest answer and it is the point. Broadband velocity is
    nearly blind to early bearing damage. The envelope spectrum sees it;
    the ISO zone does not. We report both rather than conflating them."

3. OFFLINE / AIR-GAPPED MODE  (90 s)
   Click [RCA BPFO Trip].
   "No internet is involved in that answer. The diagnosis, the risk score
    and the actions are all computed in deterministic code."
   Click [Verify LOTO & Parts] — different answer.
   Click [Stage Work Order]   — different answer again.
   Point at Output Checks 4/4 and open the kernel trace: ~15 ms total.

4. CLOUD MODE  (60 s)
   Paste your Gemini key into the box, top right. Click [RCA BPFO Trip].
   The Engine label changes from deterministic-template to the Gemini model.
   "Same diagnosis, same numbers — the model is explaining the decision,
    not making it. If it invents a number, the checks reject it and we
    fall back to the deterministic report."

5. RESILIENCE  (45 s)
   Clear the API key box. Click a chip again — answer is instant.
   "That is the circuit breaker. Under a hanging provider the original
    design took 54 seconds for every single request. This one spends its
    budget twice, then stops calling out and answers in microseconds."

6. THE OUTPUT  (30 s)
   Click "Export ISO-14224 Work Order" — a JSON file downloads.
   "SAP PM work order, staged for a planner. A human still approves it."

LIKELY QUESTIONS

Q: Is this real data?
   Yes — CWRU drive-end bearing data, 12 kHz. File 235: outer-race fault,
   0.021 inch, 1 hp load, 1771 rpm. 56 recordings, 555 one-second windows
   were used for the evaluation in the paper.

Q: How accurate is it?
   68.8% across four classes on held-out loads; 87.6% with ball faults
   excluded. Inner race 100%, outer race 86%, ball faults 0%. Ball faults
   are a known hard case on this dataset — Smith and Randall reported the
   same. We say so in the paper rather than hiding it.

Q: Why not deep learning?
   We compared. A random forest scores 96% on the usual split but drops to
   46% on defect sizes it has not seen. Adding our kinematic features takes
   it to 75%. The physics is what makes it generalise.

Q: What about the NVIDIA acceleration?
   Those are design projections and the dashboard says so. Nothing was run
   on a GPU. Measured CPU cost for the whole diagnostic path is 5.9 ms per
   one-second window, so no accelerator is needed at this scale.

Q: Could this go on a real plant?
   Not as it stands. Section VIII-G of the paper lists what is required:
   sensor mounting, bearing geometry configuration, a commissioned
   demodulation band per measurement point, historian and SAP integration,
   and an IEC 62443 network boundary.
